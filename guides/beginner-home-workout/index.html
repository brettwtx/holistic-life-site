15-Minute Beginner Home Workout (No Equipment)

Quick answer

  • Warm up for 2 minutes, do two rounds of five easy moves, then cool down for 3 minutes.
  • Every move has an easier and a harder version, so you can start where you are.
  • Aim for three workout days a week, with rest or a walk on the days between.
  • You do not need any equipment. A mat and light bands are optional extras for later.

Starting a workout routine can feel like a big step. It does not have to be. This plan takes 15 minutes, needs no equipment and uses moves that are easy on your joints. This is a nurse-run site, so every move below comes with a way to make it easier.

The goal is to finish feeling a little tired, not wiped out. You should be able to talk during each move.

Before you start

Find a spot with room to lie down flat. A sturdy chair, a wall and a countertop are all you need. Wear shoes or go barefoot on a non-slip floor. Keep water nearby.

If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or have not exercised in a long time, check with your clinician first. After that, listen to your body. Stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness or chest pain.

The format

  • Warm-up: 2 minutes
  • Workout: 2 rounds of 5 moves. Work for 30 seconds, then rest for 30 seconds. One round takes 5 minutes.
  • Cool-down: 3 minutes

If 30 seconds feels long, do 6 to 8 slow reps instead. Count the reps rather than racing the clock.

Warm-up (2 minutes)

March in place for one minute, lifting your knees as high as feels comfortable and swinging your arms. Then do arm circles for 30 seconds, small to large, forward and then back. Finish with 30 seconds of slow shoulder rolls.

The five moves

1. Sit to stand

Sit near the front edge of a sturdy chair, feet flat and hip-width apart. Lean slightly forward, press through your feet and stand up. Sit back down slowly.

  • Cue: Keep your knees pointing in line with your toes.
  • Easier: Use a higher seat or push off your thighs with your hands.
  • Harder: Lower yourself for a slow count of three, or hold a pause just above the chair.

2. Wall or counter push-up

Stand an arm's length from a wall. Place your hands on it at chest height, a little wider than your shoulders. Bend your elbows to bring your chest toward the wall, then push back.

  • Cue: Keep your body in one straight line from head to heels.
  • Easier: Stand closer to the wall.
  • Harder: Use a kitchen counter, which is lower, then a sturdy table.

3. Glute bridge

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Press through your heels and lift your hips until your body makes a line from shoulders to knees. Lower slowly.

  • Cue: Squeeze your glutes at the top without arching your lower back.
  • Easier: Lift only a few inches.
  • Harder: Hold the top for a count of three, or do it on one leg.

4. Bird dog

Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders and knees under hips. Reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back. Return, then switch sides.

  • Cue: Keep your back flat, as if a cup of water sat on it.
  • Easier: Lift just an arm, or just a leg.
  • Harder: Hold each reach for three seconds.

5. Standing calf raise

Stand tall beside a counter, holding it lightly for balance. Rise up onto the balls of your feet, pause, and lower slowly.

  • Cue: Rise straight up and avoid rolling to the outside of your feet.
  • Easier: Hold the counter with both hands.
  • Harder: Use a single hand, or lower for a slow count of three.

Cool-down (3 minutes)

Walk slowly in place for one minute to let your breathing settle. Then stretch gently for about 30 seconds each: the front of your thighs, the backs of your thighs, and your chest in a doorway. Breathe out slowly in each stretch.

A simple weekly schedule

Day Plan
Monday Workout
Tuesday Easy walk or rest
Wednesday Workout
Thursday Easy walk or rest
Friday Workout
Weekend Move for fun, or rest

The CDC says adults need 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, plus at least 2 days of muscle-strengthening activity. Three short workouts and a few walks are a good first step toward those goals. Our beginner walking plan fits neatly on the off days.

How to progress over four weeks

  1. Week 1: Two rounds, 30 seconds of work and 30 seconds of rest. Use the easier version if you need it.
  2. Week 2: Same format, with better form and slower lowering.
  3. Week 3: Work for 40 seconds and rest for 20 seconds.
  4. Week 4: Add a third round, or try one harder version.

Once those feel easy, try the beginner dumbbell workout. If you plan to build a corner for exercise, start with our small space home gym checklist.

Optional extras

You do not need to buy anything. If you want to, these small items can make the routine more comfortable.

1Exercise mat

A mat gives you a softer, non-slip surface for the floor moves, which is kinder to knees, hips and wrists, especially on hard floors.

What to look for

  • Thick enough to cushion your knees and spine
  • A textured, non-slip surface
  • Rolls up so it can live behind a door or under a bed

2Loop resistance bands

Not needed in week one. Once the moves feel easy, a light band adds a little challenge without any heavy gear.

What to look for

  • A set with light, medium and heavier strengths
  • Fabric or thick latex that does not roll or snap
  • A carry bag so they stay together

  • Easy to set work and rest times

Frequently asked questions

Is 15 minutes enough to start?

For a true beginner, yes. A short routine you repeat is more useful than a long one you skip. As moves get easier, you can add a round or a few minutes.

What if a move hurts?

Stop and switch to the easier version or skip it. Mild effort and tired muscles are normal. Sharp or joint pain is not, and it is a good reason to ask a clinician or physical therapist before you continue.
